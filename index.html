Chronicle

Checkmate & Ruin

Chess supplies the board and the geometry of movement. The RPG supplies everything chess refuses to have: hit points, growth, and consequence that isn't instant. A capture in chess removes a piece forever, in one motion, with no in-between. Here, every attack is a fight: pieces have health, deal damage ranges, sometimes miss their kill, and sometimes survive to fight again next round.

How a Round Works

Standard chess makes players alternate one move at a time, piece by piece, forever. That rule is dropped. Instead, play unfolds in rounds: on your round, every surviving piece in your company may act once, in any order: move to an open square, or step onto a square held by an enemy to fight. Once each of your pieces has acted (or you end the round early), the enemy company acts the same way, once per piece. Then a new round begins. This is closer to a tactics-RPG turn economy than to tournament chess, and it's the one deliberate rule-break that makes the fusion work: it rewards fielding a full company, not just protecting one king.

Movement

Every piece keeps its exact chess movement: pawns step forward and take diagonally, knights leap in an L, bishops and rooks slide until blocked, the queen combines both, the king steps one square in any direction. Nothing here changes. What changes is what happens when a move lands on an occupied square.

Combat, Not Capture

Moving onto an enemy-held square triggers a fight instead of an automatic removal. Damage rolls from the attacker's Might against the defender's Guard, with a small random swing. If the hit brings the defender to 0 HP, the square is taken exactly like a chess capture. If the defender survives, both pieces hold their ground. The attacker does not advance, and the defender is now wounded for next round. A weak piece can chip away at a strong one over several rounds; a strong piece can also just be worn down by numbers, the way a real skirmish goes.

The Six Orders

Growth

Winning a battle awards experience to every piece that survived it, weighted toward pieces that landed a killing blow. Experience accumulates into levels; each level raises Health, Might, and Guard by a small fixed amount. A Recruit that reaches the far rank is promoted on the spot into a Ranger, Cleric, or Warden of the player's choice, gaining that order's stat bonus immediately. The pawn-promotion rule survives from chess, but the reward is a class change rather than an automatic queen.

Ascension

At level five, a piece ascends: a permanent title and a sharpened version of its signature trait, marked on the board by a faint glow around its glyph. The Sovereign becomes a Crowned Sovereign, hardier still. The Battlemage becomes an Archmage, whose strikes ignore even more of a defender's Guard. The Warden becomes a Bulwark, whose counterattacks land at full strength instead of half. The Cleric becomes a High Cleric, whose mending restores a larger share of an ally's health. The Ranger becomes a Vanguard Ranger, landing critical strikes more often. A Recruit that ascends before promoting becomes a Veteran Recruit, tougher until the day it reaches the far rank.

The Region Map

Between battles the company travels a branching road through a region: a waypoint map of layers leading to a boss. A Battle is a standard skirmish against a generated enemy company. An Elite fields a named champion and a tougher company, but its fallen defenders leave more experience behind. A Rest waypoint costs nothing to visit and heals the entire company to full. A Shrine offers a choice of one permanent blessing (Vigor, Might, or Guard) applied to every surviving piece. A Cache holds a relic that permanently strengthens a single piece. A Wayfarer presents a short encounter with a choice attached, for better or worse. At the end of the road stands the region's Boss, a reinforced enemy company that must fall before the road can continue. Once a waypoint is chosen, the paths behind it close, and there is no backtracking to a rest missed or a shrine skipped.

Escalation

Clearing a region's boss advances the company to the next region, where a freshly generated map awaits and enemy companies field more pieces at higher levels. The run ends when your Sovereign falls, or continues indefinitely as a personal high-score of how many regions the company can clear.
